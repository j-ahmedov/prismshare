# Pilot study: simulated goodput vs colour depth and cell size

Generated by `python -m prism_share.sim.pilot --config experiments/pilot.yaml` (25 configurations, 34 conditions, 4 frames per condition, 4 decoder variants; 3.6 min on 10 cores). Full per-condition results: [`pilot_summary.csv`](pilot/pilot_summary.csv).

## Method

* **Configurations:** colour depth ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} × cell size ∈ {4, 5, 6, 8, 10} px, gap 1 px, 16 glyphs, 1024 px frames, seed 0.
* **Frames:** 4 real encoded frames per configuration (pseudo-random payload); each is degraded, quantised to 8 bits and decoded once per condition and decoder.
* **Degradations, one at a time** (`prism_share/sim/degrade.py`):
  * `blur`: Gaussian sigma (screen px); severities 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2
  * `noise`: noise sigma (8-bit code values); severities 10, 20, 40, 60, 80
  * `perspective`: max corner displacement (fraction of frame side); severities 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.3
  * `white_balance`: R/B gain imbalance (stops); severities 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4
  * `chroma_nearest`: chroma sample pitch (screen px); 2 = 4:2:0 at 1:1; severities 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4; options `{'full_range': False, 'matrix': 'bt601', 'upsample': 'nearest'}`
  * `chroma_bilinear`: chroma sample pitch (screen px); 2 = 4:2:0 at 1:1; severities 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4; options `{'full_range': False, 'matrix': 'bt601', 'upsample': 'bilinear'}`
  * `chroma_bt709_nearest`: chroma sample pitch (screen px); 2 = 4:2:0 at 1:1; severities 2; options `{'full_range': False, 'matrix': 'bt709', 'upsample': 'nearest'}`
  * `chroma_bt709_bilinear`: chroma sample pitch (screen px); 2 = 4:2:0 at 1:1; severities 2; options `{'full_range': False, 'matrix': 'bt709', 'upsample': 'bilinear'}`
* **Metrics:** shape SER and colour SER per cell, separately (`analysis/metrics.py`).
* **Goodput** = payload bytes per frame × frame yield × 30 fps (assumed, never timed). A frame counts toward yield only if every RS codeword satisfies 2·errors ≤ n − k (`analysis/ecc_sim.py`).
* **ECC:** reported two ways: the default RS(155,125), and the goodput-maximising RS(n, k) over n ∈ {155, 255} and every k (ECC is simulated, so every configuration gets its best code). Unless stated, goodput means the latter. It is an in-sample optimum over 4 frames and therefore slightly optimistic near the yield cliff.
* **Headline decoder:** `luma/saturated` (see the decoder ablation).

## Interpretation

<!-- interpretation -->
*This section is written by hand. The generator preserves it on regeneration, so re-check its numbers against the tables whenever the pilot is re-run.*

**In this simulation, the hypothesis is not supported.** A colour depth above 1 maximises goodput in 33 of 34 conditions. Monochrome wins only at a chroma pitch of 4 px with bilinear upsampling. That rung is an extrapolation: a camera coarse enough to have one chroma sample per 4 screen pixels would also have lost luma resolution, which this degradation does not model. The verdict holds under all four decoder variants, which change only the margins.

**Why colour survives each degradation on its own:**

1. **Blur, perspective and noise mostly attack shape, and they attack it almost equally at every colour depth.** Under blur σ = 1 px at 4 px cells, shape SER is 17.1 % (mono), 17.0 % (2 colours), 17.6 % (8) and 17.3 % (16). Colour decisions average over a whole cell, so they are far more robust: colour SER stays below 0.1 % up to σ = 1.25 px. The colour bits therefore come almost free, and the configuration with the most bits per cell wins.
2. **White-balance shift is cancelled by the decoder.** A gain in linear light is still a per-channel gain after the sRGB transfer function, so normalising to the frame's own white border removes it exactly, up to clipping. Only the 16-colour palette, the one that uses mid levels (128), fails, and only at ≥ 3 stops (25 % colour SER), when clipping destroys the 128/255 distinction.
3. **Chroma subsampling is the only degradation that targets colour, and its effect is large.** At pitch 2 (4:2:0 with 1:1 sampling) and 4 px cells, colour SER is 0 / 0 / 4.9 / 21.9 % for 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 colours with nearest upsampling, and 0 / 1.2 / 24.1 / 50.4 % with bilinear. The best depth falls from 16 at pitch 1, to 4 at pitch 2, to 2 at pitch 3–4. Bilinear upsampling is consistently worse for colour than nearest, because it mixes chroma across cell boundaries. Even so, 2- and 4-colour codes still beat monochrome by 1.26–1.51× at pitch 2, because luma, and with it shape, is untouched: `luma` shape reading gives 0.000 shape SER under every chroma condition.

**The decoder matters as much as the palette.** Estimating colour from the most saturated quarter of the ink pixels, rather than all of them, cuts mean colour SER under 4:2:0 by 1.8–6.7× (see the ablation), because the pixels chroma subsampling dilutes least are the most saturated ones. The price under strong noise is steep, because there the noisiest pixels look the most saturated. For 16 colours at noise σ = 60, averaged over cell sizes, colour SER is 21.3 % with `saturated` against 1.3 % with `mean`. The headline decoder therefore understates colour in the high-noise rungs. Under `max/mean` the noise winners at σ = 40 / 60 / 80 are 16 / 16 / 8 colours (8.97 / 7.97 / 6.64 Mbit/s), instead of 8 / 2 / 2 colours (7.33 / 5.64 / 5.46). No single variant is best everywhere. A receiver could run all of them and keep whichever passes RS + CRC; the pilot does not credit that.

**Cell size is set by blur, independently of colour depth.** The goodput-maximising cell size is the same at every colour depth: 4 px up to σ = 0.75, 5 px at σ = 1, 6 px at σ = 1.25–1.5, and 10 px at σ = 2. That is roughly cell_px ≈ 4–5 σ. Under every other degradation the smallest cell (4 px) wins. In this model, cell size and colour depth are close to separable design choices.

**One palette weakness is visible.** The 4-colour palette (R, G, B, W) is the outlier in shape: 24 % shape SER at blur σ = 1, 4 px, against 17 % for the others, and 16 % at noise σ = 80 against < 0.4 %. Pure blue has only 11 % luma, so its shape signal is weak and a bright blurred neighbour swamps it. The palette rule maximises RGB distance and ignores luma contrast. A luma-aware rule would probably remove this.

**What this simulation does not contain, and why the capture study can still overturn it:**

* **Bayer mosaic and demosaicing.** The hypothesis names demosaicing explicitly, and it is not simulated here. At 1:1 sampling, demosaicing produces false colour at every glyph edge. It is the most likely candidate for making colour lose, and it can be simulated as a sixth degradation (mosaic + bilinear demosaic), the synthetic twin of the RAW path. *Recommended before capture.*
* **Combined degradations.** Real frames are blurred *and* chroma-subsampled *and* noisy. Blur spreads each cell's colour into its neighbours before 4:2:0 dilutes it further, so single-degradation results are an upper bound for colour.
* **Realistic noise.** Camera noise is signal-dependent and per channel, and AWB gains amplify it in the blue channel. Here it is additive, white and equal in every channel.
* **Display and compression effects.** Sub-pixel layout, panel crosstalk, display-sensor moiré and video compression are all absent. If captures ever pass through H.264/HEVC, compression will dominate colour loss.
* **Optimistic ECC.** The goodput-maximising code is chosen on the same 4 frames it is scored on and often has very little parity (RS(255,254) where no errors were seen). A deployed system needs margin; this favours whichever configuration is closest to error-free, which here is colour.
* **Pessimistic chroma pitch.** A phone filming the 1024 px code at 1080p–4K samples each screen pixel with ~1.3–2.6 sensor pixels, so its effective chroma pitch is ~0.8–1.5. On this ladder that is where colour's advantage is largest.

**Prediction for the capture study.** If monochrome wins on real hardware, the cause must be something outside this model: demosaicing, interacting degradations, or real noise. The YUV / demosaic / no-demosaic comparison in the mechanism experiment is therefore the decisive measurement. The pilot predicts that YUV colour SER will track the effective chroma pitch, and that bilinear upsampling will hurt colour more than nearest.
